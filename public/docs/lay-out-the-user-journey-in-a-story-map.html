Lay out the user journey in a story map

1 min read

A user story map starts with people, not tickets. Who is the software for, and what are they trying to do?

For the coffee app, that's a customer who wants to order a coffee. Ordering a coffee takes a few steps: browse the menu, customise a drink, add it to the cart, pay, then confirm the order.

Put those along the top, in order, and each ticket goes under the step it belongs to.

The rows along the top have names:

  1. 1Users are who the product is for.
  2. 2Activities are what they're trying to do.
  3. 3Steps are how they do it, from left to right.

Together, they're the backbone of the story map. Read it from left to right and it tells the story of the product: the customer browses the menu, customises a drink, adds it to the cart, pays and confirms the order.

Then what? The story stops once the order is in. There's no step for collecting the coffee, and no way of knowing when it's ready. A whole step is missing. In the list, that gap was invisible. On the story map, it's an empty space you can't miss.
