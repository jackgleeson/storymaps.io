Story map glossary

5 min read

On this page

The terms used on a user story map and in these docs. Start with the picture, then find any term in the list at the end.

The parts of a story map

  1. 1Users are the people the product is for.
  2. 2Activities are the big things they're trying to do.
  3. 3Steps break the journey down, from left to right. Activities and steps together are the backbone of the story map.
  4. 4Tasks are the Yellow cards under each step, with the most important at the top. Each is a feature or task to implement so that someone can do that step.
  5. 5Slices run across the story map and group the tasks into releases. The first is often the MVP.

Kinds of card

Under the steps, the colour of a card says what kind of card it is. A new story map starts with these four.

TaskA feature or task to implement.
NoteA comment about a feature, task or step.
QuestionInformation needed.
Edge caseWhat happens when things do not go to plan.

The names live in the legend. You are free to rename them or add your own.

Agile terms and story map terms

Agile teams and their trackers have their own vocabulary. Find a term on the left to see what it is called on a story map.

Agile termOn a story mapWhat it is
Ticket, issue, work item, storyTaskA feature or task to implement, under a step. Tickets you import from Jira, Linear, Azure DevOps, Asana or Phabricator come in as tasks.
Box, sticky note, itemCardAny box on the story map. Users, activities, steps and tasks are all cards.
Release, sprint, iteration, milestone, swimlaneSliceA row of cards to build together. It runs across every step.
Persona, role, actorUserWho the story map is for. Users sit in the top row.
Goal, theme, stageActivityA big thing a user sets out to do. It spans one or more steps.
Column, stage of the journeyStepOne thing the user does on their journey, in order.

Storymaps.io tools

The parts of Storymaps.io itself, as opposed to the parts of a story map.

  1. 1The open tab shows above the tabs. Here it is the Overview: counts, progress for each slice, and the legend.
  2. 2The tabs are Overview, Partials, Notes and Log. Click a tab to open it, and click it again to close it.
  3. 3The view controls are focus mode, full screen, dark mode, cursors and freeze rows.
  4. 4Zoom makes the story map bigger or smaller.
Control panel
The block at the bottom right of a story map: four tabs, with the view controls and zoom under them.
Overview
A tab of the control panel. It counts the cards, steps, slices, users, activities and partials, shows progress for each slice, and holds the legend. Review release progress on a story map
Partials
A tab of the control panel. It lists the partials on this story map. Reduce duplication with partials
Notepad
Free text that everyone on the story map shares, for anything that does not fit on a card. It opens from the Notes tab of the control panel. Edit a story map together
Log
A tab of the control panel. It keeps a running record of who has been on the story map, what they changed and when. Track changes to a story map
View controls
The row under the tabs: focus mode, full screen, dark mode, cursors and freeze rows. Change the story map view
Zoom
The plus and minus buttons beside the view controls. They make the story map bigger or smaller. Zoom and pan around a story map

All the terms

Activity
A big thing a user sets out to do. It spans one or more steps.Also called: goal, theme
Backbone
The activities and steps together. Read from left to right, they tell the story of the product. The users sit above the backbone. Define the story map backbone
Backup
A copy of the whole story map at one moment, to go back to if a change goes wrong. Back up and restore a story map
Card
Any box on the story map. Users, activities, steps, tasks, notes, questions and edge cases are all cards.Also called: sticky note, item
Edge case
A Rose card: what happens when things do not go to plan.Also called: exception
Future story map
The classic kind: the product you are about to build, laid out as the journey people will take through it. Future story map
Legend
The list that names each card colour, so everyone reads the colours the same way. It is in the Overview tab.Also called: key
Lock
Makes a story map read-only for everyone without the password. Lock a story map
Narrative flow
The left to right order of the steps: the order you would tell the story in.
Note
A Cyan card: a comment about a feature, task or step.
Now story map
A story map of how something works today, with its problems marked. Now story mapAlso called: journey map, as-is story map
Partial
A run of steps kept in one place and used wherever the journey needs them, such as checkout. Reduce duplication with partials
Project
A set of related story maps kept together as tabs, under one link. Start a story map project
Question
A Lime card: information needed.
Slice
A row of cards to build together, such as an MVP or a release. It runs across every step. Plan releases with story map slicesAlso called: release slice, release, iteration, swimlane
Status
How far along a task is: planned, in progress, done or blocked. Customise story map cards
Step
One thing the user does on their journey, in order. Each step is a column. A step is not a task: the tasks are the Yellow cards under it.
Story map
The whole board: the journey across the top and the tasks under it, cut into slices. What is a user story map?Also called: user story map, USM
Tag
A short label on a card, such as design or dev. Customise story map cardsAlso called: label
Task
A Yellow card under a step: a feature or task to implement so that someone can do that step. A ticket or issue from a tracker comes in as a task. Add tasks to a story mapAlso called: story, ticket, issue, work item
Technical story map
A story map where the users are systems, such as an app or a payment provider, and the steps are the calls they make. Technical story map
User
Who the story map is for.Also called: persona, role
Walking skeleton
The thinnest slice that still works from the first step to the last.Also called: MVP, when it is the first release
