Run a user story mapping session

6 min read

On this page

The first story map works best when the team builds it together. Everyone adds what they know, and everyone leaves with the same picture of the product and a first release they agreed on. New to story maps? What is a user story map? explains the parts.

Who to invite

Invite the people who'll shape and build the product:

  • Product, to lead the session and set out the goal.
  • Engineering, to say what's feasible, and flag what's risky or cheaper another way.
  • Design, to share what they've learned about users and shape the experience.
  • Key stakeholders, such as the sponsor or a department head, to agree the goal and back the first release.
  • Anyone close to the users, such as support or sales, to say what users ask for and struggle with.

Bring a short brief if there is one: who the product is for and why it's worth building. How story mapping changes a product team explains where a brief fits in.

Before you start

  1. 1Create a user story map and name it.
  2. 2Copy the link from Share → Share URL and send it to everyone, so the whole team edits the same story map. Changes show for everyone as they happen. The first time someone arrives, the app asks for their name, so everyone can see who's who.

How you run it depends on where everyone is:

  • In person: put the story map on the big screen, and everyone works on it from their own device.
  • Remote: the person leading shares their screen, and everyone else works on it from their own device.

If everyone's cursors moving around distract whoever's presenting, they can click Hide external cursors in the view controls at the bottom right. It only changes their own view. Change the story map view has more.

Tip. Put questions on the story map as Lime cards, the legend's colour for questions, under the step they're about. Use the Notes tab for decisions and anything that doesn't belong to one step. Everyone with the link sees the same notes.

1. Agree who it's for

Start with the users row. Name the people the product is for, such as Customer and Barista. Settle any disagreement here first, since everything else on the story map depends on who it's for.

2. Walk the journey

Lay out the backbone from left to right: the activities, then the steps under each. Tell it as the story of one user when everything goes right (the happy path): first they do this, then that. Keep it high-level for now, and get the whole journey down before anyone goes into detail on one step.

Then read it back out loud, from left to right. If one step doesn't lead to the next, there's a step missing.

Define the story map backbone shows how to add and move users, activities and steps.

Tip. When someone raises something that could go wrong, or an unusual case, add it as a Rose card under that step, the legend's colour for edge cases, and keep going.

3. Fill in the tasks

Under each step, everyone adds the tasks they think are needed to complete it as Yellow cards, the legend's colour for tasks, and any good ideas for the future. Everyone can do this at the same time.

Don't slice releases yet. To show what's for now and what's for later, move the tasks for now to the top of the column.

4. Review the tasks together

With everyone adding at once, expect some repeats. Go through the story map together from left to right, one step at a time, and talk through what's there. Merge tasks that overlap, remove duplicates, and reword any task that isn't clear to everyone. If the group can't settle something yet, turn it into a Lime question card and move on.

Then sort each column, with the most important task at the top. If someone moves a card and someone else moves it back, stop and talk about why. Add tasks to a story map covers adding and moving them.

Note. The conversation is as valuable as the story map. As Jeff Patton puts it: "Use the creation of the model as an opportunity to elicit discussion about the features being built." (It's All in How You Slice, Jeff Patton.)

5. Slice a first release

Decide the first release together. Go through the steps from left to right and ask what's the minimum implementation each one needs to work and be useful. Move the tasks that cover it into the first slice, so users can get from the first step to the last in the simplest way that works. Then give the slice a goal everyone agrees on, such as "Customers can order and pay ahead, then pick up their coffee", and a target date to make it real.

Then deal with the rest. If the remaining work is already prioritised, slice it into later releases. If not, put it all in one Later slice to come back to. Nothing needs deleting.

The session is done when the team agrees on the first slice, usually the MVP or V1, and who's picking up what next. Slice an MVP with a story map explains why the slice runs across the whole journey, and Plan releases with story map slices shows how to add one and give it a goal and a date.

After the session

The story map is where the plan lives from now on:

  • Mark the milestone with a backup, with a note such as "After the first session", so you can always get back to what the team agreed.
  • Build the first slice as a prototype to learn from users before the team commits to it. Build with AI shows how.
  • Turn the first slice into tickets with the team. The Jira, Asana, Phabricator and Linear integrations can send the cards as epics or tasks, to save copying them across by hand.
  • Share the link wherever the team talks about the work, such as a chat or a ticket.
  • Add new ideas and feedback as they come in, under the step they relate to, so they're there when you plan the next release.
  • Come back to it to plan each release.
  • See how a release is going in Review release progress on a story map.

Split the work by team

Tag each card with the team that will build it, such as mobile or payments, so each team can see its own work.

  1. 1Select the cards for one team, by dragging a box around them or with Shift+click.
  2. 2Click Tags on the toolbar, type the team name in New tag... and press Enter.
  3. 3Do the same for each team.

To see the work for one team, click the funnel beside the search box, tick the tag under Tags and click Done. The cards for that team stay bright in every step and slice. Filters only change what you see, so each team can view its own work at the same time. Search and filter a story map covers the rest, including searching within the tagged cards.
