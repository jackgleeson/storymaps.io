Why user story mapping

It empowers you and your team to deliver impactful work faster than ever.

User story mapping drives collaboration, discovery, insight, and ruthless prioritisation. It requires everyone involved to shape the project, understand its objectives, and share in its impact. It strengthens cross-department relationships, saves time, reduces waste, and helps you release faster.

Read how it works

What changes for you

Product

You're no longer the only one holding the plan. Instead of writing the requirements first and walking everyone through them, you lead the planning session where you and the team map the product. Everyone leaves knowing what comes first and why, motivated because they have a stake in it. When it's time to write tickets, you and the team start from the tasks on the story map. The story map stays in use from that first meeting right the way through, evolving along with the product.

Engineering

No more being brought in once the big decisions are made. You're in the room when the product is mapped, so you can flag what's risky, or cheaper another way, before anything is promised. When it's time to write tickets, you and the product team start from the tasks on the story map, and each release slice says what that release needs. Once it's agreed, the work is focused, minimising scope creep. Because you can see the whole journey, today's choices won't box you in later. You helped make the trade-offs, so you know why your work matters.

Design

There's no need to design the whole product before anyone has used any of it. You're in the room when the journey is mapped, so what you know about users shapes the plan from the start. Each release slice shows you and the product team which steps to design next, and because you can see the whole journey, they still join up into one experience. Flows months away can wait until people have used what you shipped. With the slice agreed, you design what's in it, not everything that might come later.

Leadership

A plan the team builds together is one everyone supports. People who helped shape the plan feel empowered to act on it and motivated to deliver it. You can see the whole product in one place: what each release is for, what's in it and how far along it is, without chasing status updates. What people know ends up on the story map for everyone to see. When priorities change, you can see what moves before anyone commits to it.

Founders

The product no longer lives only in your head. Mapping it lays the whole idea out where your team, contractors and investors can see it. The first release slice shows the smallest thing worth launching, so you spend your time and money proving the idea, not building everything at once. As you learn from customers, the story map changes with you, and everyone you bring on can see where the product is going and why.
