How a story map helps teams decide what to build

2 min read

On this page

A user story map can start before there's an idea. The team maps how things work today, finds the problems worth fixing, and tests ideas before committing to them. By the time the plan is agreed, everyone has seen why.

Find the problems worth fixing

A now story map lays out how something works today. Walk the journey with the people who do it every day, step by step, including the workarounds. Then mark the problems or pain points under the step where they happen, in a colour of their own.

Note. A workaround, such as a shortcut only a few people know or a requirement to look up critical data elsewhere, is a missing feature, so give each one a card. People working around the product aren't misusing it, they're showing what it's missing.

The steps where problems pile up are the place to look first. A step with five problems under it says more than a list of complaints, because everyone can see where in the journey each one happens, and what comes before and after it.

Try it now

Open the morning rush at a coffee shop, mapped as it works today, with its problems and opportunities.

Open practice story map

Catch ideas where they belong

Ideas come up as soon as people see the problems laid out. Put each one under the step it would improve, in its own colour. An idea next to the problem it fixes is easier to weigh up than one on a list.

Bring in what you already know

Interviews, support requests and usage data all fit on the story map. Put each finding under the step it's about. A problem that turns up in interviews and in the data deserves more attention than one that turns up once. Anything nobody can answer yet goes on as a question to follow up.

Shape the idea

A future story map shapes the idea. Copy the now story map, keep the steps that still make sense, change the ones that don't, and turn the opportunities worth taking into tasks.

Test it before you commit

Some ideas only show their problems once people try them. Build a quick prototype of one slice, show it to the people who'll use it, and change the story map with what you learn. Build with AI turns a slice into a working prototype, so trying an idea costs little.

Decide what to build first

Slice an MVP: take the smallest set of tasks that gets someone through the whole journey, and give the slice a goal. The goal says which problems the release fixes, so anyone can trace the decision back to the now story map.

With or without a write-up

If there's already a write-up, bring it along and map from it. If there isn't, you don't need one: the team works out what to build and why as they map. Either way, the story map turns that into an ordered, sliced plan the whole team can start on.
