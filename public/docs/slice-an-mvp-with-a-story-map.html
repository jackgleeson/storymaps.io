Slice an MVP with a story map

2 min read

You can't build everything at once, so you need a first release. There are two ways to pick one.

The first is one step at a time: finish browsing, then customising, then the cart, and so on.

The second is release slicing: go through the steps from left to right and ask what's the minimum implementation each one needs to work and be useful. Take only the tasks that cover it, so users can get from the first step to the last in the simplest way that works.

On a story map, a slice is a row across every step that groups tasks into a release. The first is often called the MVP, the minimum viable product: the smallest version that's still useful. Everything else waits in a later slice.

Thin slices get something real in front of people sooner. A coffee app that only lets you browse items is a menu. One that lets you order a plain coffee is a new revenue stream. You can also see what its first customers order, where they give up and what they ask for, which shows you what to build next.

Note. Experience tells us that not all of our ideas are good, and not everything we build gets used. So instead of trying to predict what will succeed, focus on testing assumptions quickly and cheaply. Getting something out sooner shows us what works before we spend too long on the wrong thing, and if we have to throw something away, it hasn't cost much.
